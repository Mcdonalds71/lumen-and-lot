# Journal fact check

The three articles keep to general facts, but a few statements are specific enough to check
before launch. Each line names the article, the claim and what to confirm.

## Designing for daylight

- [ ] Copenhagen: noon sun about 11° at the winter solstice and about 58° at the summer solstice.
      Day length about 7 hours in December and over 17 hours in June.
- [ ] Lisbon: noon sun about 28° at the winter solstice and about 75° at the summer solstice.
- [ ] Rule of thumb: useful daylight reaches about twice the window head height into a room.
      Sources give anything from 1.5 to 2.5 times; "about twice" sits in that range.

## How to read an energy certificate

- [ ] EnerGuide (Natural Resources Canada): the current label shows estimated energy use in
      gigajoules per year, lower is better, beside a reference house built to typical new-home
      standards. Older labels used a 0 to 100 scale where higher was better.
- [ ] HERS Index (RESNET): 100 is the reference home, 0 is a net zero energy home, and each point
      is roughly one percent.
- [ ] Energimærke (Denmark): required when a home is sold or rented. Scale A2020, A2015, A2010,
      B to G. The label comes with a report of suggested improvements.
- [ ] Certificado Energético (Portugal): managed by ADENE, required for a sale or rental, classes
      A+, A, B, B-, C to F, and new homes must reach at least B-.
- [ ] Certificates are valid for a fixed number of years in each system (stated only in general).

## Buying a new home from abroad

- [ ] Canada: the federal ban on most purchases of residential property by non-Canadians has been
      in place since January 1, 2023, with exceptions. Check whether it has been extended past
      January 1, 2027.
- [ ] Denmark: people who do not live in Denmark generally need permission to buy property, with
      exceptions, including for many EU citizens.
- [ ] The United States and Portugal place fewer limits on who can buy a home. Some US states
      have passed restrictions on buyers from certain countries.
- [ ] Portugal: a tax number (NIF) is needed before signing.
- [ ] New-build deposits are often held in a protected account or covered by a guarantee
      (stated as "in many places").
